Why You Might Want to Contact Me

A website becomes more useful when there is a genuine connection between the person writing and the people reading. I do not want Kinglee Mutwiri to be a place where articles are simply published and forgotten. Questions, corrections and thoughtful disagreement can make an article better.

You may contact me if something you read on the website was unclear, if you want to discuss an idea, if you have another perspective on a story, or if you believe an important detail needs to be corrected.

You can also get in touch if you have a suggestion for a future article. A good article often begins with a simple question that deserves more attention than it normally receives.

One important principle: if you are contacting me about a factual error, please include the page and explain what you believe is incorrect. This makes it much easier to investigate the issue properly rather than making a change based only on an assumption.

For Article Corrections

Accuracy matters. Published information can sometimes contain an error, an unclear statement, an outdated detail or a point that needs stronger context. If you notice something like this, please tell me.

When reporting a possible correction, it helps to provide the article title or page address, the part you believe needs attention, and the information you believe is more accurate.

A correction request does not mean that every disagreement will result in an immediate change. Information should be checked before a factual statement is changed. The aim is to improve accuracy, not to change an article simply because somebody has a different opinion.

If you have a reliable reference that helps establish the correct information, you can mention it in your message. This is particularly useful for dates, names, statistics, historical events and other factual details.

General Feedback

Feedback can be positive, negative or somewhere in between. Useful criticism is valuable because it shows what a reader noticed that the writer may have missed.

You can tell me if an article was useful, if an explanation was too complicated, if a section needed more context, or if there is a topic you think deserves a deeper article.

I also welcome thoughtful disagreement. Not every reader will reach the same conclusion from a story or argument, and that is part of having a meaningful conversation about ideas.

What matters is that the conversation remains respectful and useful. A different opinion can improve thinking when it is explained rather than simply stated.

Suggest an Article Topic

Some of the strongest article ideas begin with questions people ask in ordinary conversations. If there is a person, story, behaviour, financial idea, life question or practical subject you think deserves attention, you can suggest it.

A useful suggestion does not need to be complicated. You can simply explain what you want to understand and why you think other readers might also benefit from the answer.

Suggestions are considered based on whether they fit the purpose of the website and whether there is enough reliable information to produce something genuinely useful.

Before You Send a Message

A clear message usually receives a better response because it makes the purpose of the conversation easy to understand.

For a correction

Include the article title, the specific statement you are questioning, and the corrected information if you have it.

For feedback

Tell me what worked or did not work for you. If possible, explain what could make the article more useful to another reader.

For an article idea

Explain the question behind the idea. The more clearly the question is defined, the easier it is to understand what kind of article could answer it.

For a website problem

Tell me which page you were visiting and describe what went wrong. If a link does not work or something appears incorrectly, the page address and a short explanation are useful.

When You Can Expect a Response

Messages are not guaranteed to receive an immediate response. The time required can depend on the nature of the question, whether research is needed, and how many messages are waiting for attention.

Simple questions may be easier to answer than detailed correction requests that require checking information. For that reason, it is better to allow reasonable time for a considered response.

If you are reporting an important factual problem, make that clear in the message and provide enough information for the issue to be investigated.
