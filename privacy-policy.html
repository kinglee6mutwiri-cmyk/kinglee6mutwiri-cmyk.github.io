18. Children and Young Visitors

Children and young people should learn to protect personal information when using the internet. Parents and guardians can play an important role by discussing safe online communication and explaining why private information should not be shared unnecessarily.

Website Eliphas does not require ordinary visitors to submit sensitive personal information simply to read public articles.

If a parent or guardian believes that a child has accidentally provided inappropriate personal information through a website communication channel, the parent or guardian may contact the website to explain the concern.

The safest approach for young internet users is to avoid sharing passwords, addresses, financial details, account credentials and other sensitive information with people or services that do not need that information.

19. Data Security

Protecting information is important, but no internet communication method can honestly be described as completely risk-free. Websites, email systems, messaging platforms, networks and devices can all experience technical problems.

For this reason, visitors should exercise reasonable caution before sending information online.

Website Eliphas encourages visitors to minimise the amount of sensitive information they share. This principle reduces risk because information that is never unnecessarily transmitted does not need to be protected after transmission.

Visitors should also protect their own devices with reasonable security practices, including keeping software updated, using strong passwords and avoiding suspicious links or messages.

Website security is therefore a shared responsibility between technology providers, website operators and users.

20. Data Retention

Email providers, messaging platforms and social-media services may retain information according to their own policies and technical requirements.

21. Corrections and Privacy Concerns

Visitors may contact the website if they believe a published page contains information that should not have been made public or if they have a legitimate privacy concern about information connected to the website.

Visitors should not respond to a privacy concern by sending additional sensitive information unnecessarily. If a concern can be explained without attaching a confidential document, that is normally preferable.

22. Requests to Review Information

23. Accuracy of Information

Privacy and accuracy are closely connected. A person may have a legitimate interest in ensuring that information about them is not presented incorrectly.

The purpose of receiving correction requests is to encourage responsible publishing and reduce the continued presentation of information that may be inaccurate.

27. Responsible Online Communication

Privacy is not only about what websites collect. It is also about how people communicate online.

A visitor who wants to report a problem should provide enough information to explain the problem without exposing unrelated private details.

A visitor who wants to suggest an article can describe the idea without providing personal information that has nothing to do with the suggestion.

A visitor who wants to ask a question can normally do so without sending confidential credentials.

This simple approach helps everyone communicate more safely.

28. Information Security for Visitors

Visitors should also protect themselves when browsing the internet. A privacy policy cannot protect a visitor who voluntarily provides their password to a stranger or enters confidential information into a fraudulent website.

Visitors should check website addresses before entering sensitive information. They should be careful with unexpected messages and should avoid clicking suspicious links.

Passwords should be unique and difficult to guess. Account security codes should never be shared casually.

Keeping the operating system and browser updated can also help reduce security risks.

Visitors who are particularly concerned about privacy can review the privacy controls provided by their browser and operating system.

35. Data Minimisation

One of the simplest privacy principles is data minimisation. This means avoiding the unnecessary collection or sharing of information.

Website communication can often be completed with very little personal information. A question about an article does not normally require a person's identification document. A technical complaint does not normally require banking details.

Visitors are therefore encouraged to provide only information that helps achieve the purpose of the communication.

This principle can reduce privacy risks while making communication more efficient.

Clear requests help reduce confusion and make it easier to determine what issue needs to be considered.
